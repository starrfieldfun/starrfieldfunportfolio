PROJECT 06 / 2026

PikStiq.

From sticker idea to business system.

PikStiq started as a sticker business idea and grew into a connected digital system: a storefront for ready-made packs and personalised orders, plus the internal tools needed to price, manage, produce and fulfil each order.

ROLEFounder · UI/UX Designer · Developer
TYPESticker Commerce Platform
FOCUSCustomer UX + Business Operations
STACKHTML · CSS · JS · Cloudflare
Challenge Journey Customer experience Functions Operations Systems Outcome

01 / CHALLENGE

Not just a sticker website. A whole small business system.

PikStiq needed to help different kinds of customers order correctly while also giving the business a manageable way to handle artwork, pricing, production, stock, delivery and follow-up.

01Reduce ordering confusion

Separate very different jobs instead of forcing everyone into one generic form.

02Translate physical constraints

Make sizes, minimum quantities, sheet capacity and delivery feel understandable online.

03Support the operator

Turn every paid order into a clear production task for a founder-run workflow.

02 / WHAT WAS DONE

Built step by step, around the real workflow.

FOUNDATION

Created the brand and product direction

Defined PikStiq as a glossy sticker brand, shaped its visual identity, product offer and launch presentation, and tested the physical production setup: printable vinyl, laminate, Cricut cutting and the practical sizing rules behind each order.

STOREFRONT

Designed and built the customer website

Created the public storefront with a warm, approachable visual style, product discovery, custom ordering, a calculator, FAQs, policies and a checkout flow aligned to Singapore delivery and payment expectations.

SIMPLIFICATION

Separated the three service paths

Restructured the products experience so customers clearly choose between Ready-Made Stickers, Print My Design and Custom Design. Each path asks for the right inputs and only the right inputs.

COMMERCE RULES

Made pricing and delivery logic usable

Mapped standard sizes, custom dimensions, minimum quantities, sales, discounts and delivery estimates into a clearer UI so customers can understand what they are buying before payment.

OPERATIONS

Added admin tools and production workflow

Built admin flows for products, orders, queue visibility, pricing controls, campaign tools and later-documented supply tracking so the business side stays practical instead of relying on scattered manual notes.

03 / CUSTOMER EXPERIENCE

Start from customer intent, not internal complexity.

PATH 01

Ready-Made Stickers

For customers who want to browse finished sticker packs, choose a size, order complete collections or build a mix-and-match set from individual designs.

  • Published catalogue and category filtering
  • Pack pages with gallery, sizes and stock messaging
  • Mix-and-match selection inside the ready-made flow
PATH 02

Print My Design

For customers who already have a completed artwork file and simply want PikStiq to print it as stickers.

  • Size and quantity first
  • Artwork upload without style questions
  • Review and payment for print-only jobs
PATH 03

Custom Design

For customers who want stickers illustrated from their photos or references.

  • Photo upload and style selection
  • Optional design notes
  • Artwork approval before production

This three-path structure came from documented product iteration and was reinforced in the reviewed v0.51.22 release notes.

04 / FUNCTIONS & FEATURES

What the product actually does. Front stage and back stage.

Storefront

Homepage, products page, calculator, FAQ, contact flow, order tracking and supporting informational pages.

Product discovery

Ready-made packs, complete collections, category filters, individual design browsing and image-led product modals.

Size & quantity logic

Standard 4, 6, 8 and 10 cm sizes, custom dimension handling, same-size or mixed-size selection, MOQ guidance and size helper visuals.

Pricing system

Separate rules for custom stickers, ready-made packs and mix-and-match, plus discounts, sales tools and delivery estimate support.

Checkout

Singapore-focused address fields, order summaries, customer details, legal consent and secure payment handoff through HitPay.

Tracking & communication

Order references, progress communication and documented email/notification routines tied to the business workflow.

05 / THE OPERATING SIDE

The website is only half the job. The rest is operations design.

ADMIN SYSTEM

How the business side works

Founder-run workflow
ORDERS

Order management

See service type, customer details, uploaded artwork, delivery information and current status in one place.

QUEUE

Production queue

A read-only view of paid online orders to clarify what is ready to work on, without introducing a second disconnected tracking system.

SAFEGUARDS

Production rules

Documented safeguards prevent unpaid orders from entering production and stop print-only jobs from moving through illustration stages.

CATALOGUE

Products & pricing

Manage ready-made listings, size prices, sales, discount percentages and customer-facing pricing updates.

SUPPLIES

Stock tracking

Later documented development added supply tracking for sticker paper, laminate, blade wear and CMYK ink so material usage becomes easier to monitor.

FULFILMENT

Delivery flow

Supports estimated Monday and Wednesday runs, cutoffs, order tracking and customer expectation setting.

Important note

Some admin functions above were reviewed directly in saved source or release notes, while some later additions are drawn from recorded project development history and are described as documented features rather than newly verified live screens.

07 / OUTCOME

More than an online shop. A business system in motion.

PikStiq shows how I think beyond surface visuals. I had to connect product positioning, customer clarity, checkout logic, backend rules, production operations and the realities of a small physical business.

It is also a good example of iterative design: the project kept evolving whenever the workflow exposed friction — whether that meant clarifying service choices, changing pricing logic, improving admin visibility or adding tools that made daily operations easier.

KEY TAKEAWAY Designing the interface was only part of the work.

What made PikStiq valuable was designing the system around the business behind it.
